# OT Security Context

## The problem

Operational technology networks control physical processes. Unlike IT networks, where a breach means data loss, an OT breach can mean physical damage, environmental release, or loss of life.

## Common exposure patterns

1. **Flat networks**: IT and OT on the same segment with no segmentation
2. **Default credentials**: devices shipped with admin/admin or blank passwords, never changed
3. **Unpatched firmware**: devices running versions with known CVEs, sometimes years old
4. **Internet-facing gateways**: VPN concentrators or remote-access appliances bridging OT to the public internet
5. **Legacy protocols**: Modbus, DNP3, and BACnet were designed with no authentication model

## Why scanning matters

You cannot defend what you cannot see. Asset inventory is the foundation of every OT security program. This scanner produces that inventory with vulnerability context attached.

## Regulatory landscape

- **NERC CIP**: mandatory standards for bulk electric system operators
- **IEC 62443**: industrial automation and control systems security framework
- **NIST SP 800-82**: guide to operational technology security
- **CISA alerts**: ongoing advisories on exposed ICS devices

## Career relevance

OT security is one of the most understaffed domains in cybersecurity. The intersection of network engineering, industrial control, and adversarial thinking is rare — and in demand at utilities, manufacturers, defense contractors, and government agencies.
